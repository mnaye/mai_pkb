## The week in one paragraph

A week after Anthropic and OpenAI competed on price and turn count, **Google answered with Gemini 4 Argon** (**30 September**) — and shipped it the way frontier labs now ship their strongest models: **to cyber defenders first**, through the Fairwind Program, with paid API and Ultra access promised "as soon as possible" and no general-availability date. The engineering headline is the **1 million output-token limit** (up from 64K), aimed squarely at long-horizon agentic work, plus introductory pricing of **$2 / $10 per M tokens** that rises to **$4 / $20** — the same list price as Claude Opus 5.5. The day before, **OpenAI launched dots**, always-on GPT-6 Astra agents that each get their own cloud computer and keep working after the chat closes. Together they make the week's through-line plain: the frontier is now selling **long-running, persistent agents**, and access is being gated by risk rather than by price. On GitHub, **`vectorize-io/hindsight`** held the agent-memory story for a second week (**45.8k ★, +10,623**), and multi-agent "team" harnesses kept climbing. ## Items

- **[Gemini 4 Argon](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-4-argon/)** `topic:models` — Google's new frontier model, announced **30 September 2026**, built for "complex, long-horizon workflows" in software engineering, enterprise knowledge work (legal, finance) and cybersecurity defense. **Availability is gated:** it is rolling out first to "trusted cyber defenders" in Google's **Fairwind Program**, with broader access to come "starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers" — no date given. **Output limit rises to 1 million tokens** (from 64K); the post does not state an input context window. Google-reported scores: **DeepSWE v1.1 77.9%**, **AutomationBench 51.3% (#1)**, **CWE-bench v1 68%** on vulnerability remediation (tied for first), **LVBench 91.7%** on long video, and leading results on the **Vals Index** (finance, coding, legal, tax) and on **Gray Swan IPI** prompt-injection robustness. **Pricing:** introductory **$2 / M input, $10 / M output**, then **$4 / $20**, with cached input at **95% off**. Safeguards include monitoring of internal activations for misuse and of chain-of-thought and actions for misalignment. The post's only healthcare mention: a partner (Wiz) used it to find "a critical vulnerability exposing sensitive personal information across healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide." *Primary post fetched 2026-10-05. All benchmark figures are Google's own; not independently checked.*

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** **Gemini 4 Argon** can write very long answers, up to 1 million tokens of output. That could mean whole reports or long code in one go. But you probably can't use it yet. Google is giving it to security teams first. Plan your test now, but don't change your plans around it until you get access.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** Google says Argon resists prompt injection well. That is when hidden text in a document tricks the AI into doing something bad. Any agent that reads patient notes, faxes or emails faces this risk. Google's scores are its own. When you get access, test it on your own tricky documents. Also check if Google will sign a HIPAA business associate agreement (a contract that lets a vendor handle patient data) for this model before any real data goes in.
- **Healthcare executives:** Google found a serious security hole in hospital software with Argon's help. Attackers will get tools like this too. Ask your security lead how fast you can patch the systems that hold patient data. Price is not the gate anymore. At **$4 / $20 per million tokens**, Argon costs the same as Claude Opus 5.5. The top labs now decide who gets their best models based on risk.

### Problems worth taking on

**Find security holes in your own code first.** *Problem:* Hospital IT teams patch slowly, and patient data sits in old in-house code. *Why now:* Google says **Argon** helped find a serious flaw in hospital software, and it scores well on fixing known weakness types. *First step:* Pick one in-house app that touches patient data. Run an AI code review you already have access to on it. Count real flaws found and how many hours it took your security team to confirm them.

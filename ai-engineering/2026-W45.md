## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** **GPT-6.1 Terra** costs $1 for every million tokens (small chunks of text) it reads, and $5 per million it writes. That is half the price of the models it sits beside. Big jobs, like pulling facts from thousands of notes, may now cost much less. Re-run one of your past extraction tests on Terra. Compare its accuracy and cost to the model you use today.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** OpenAI's speed and error numbers for **Terra** come from its own tests. Check them on your own tasks before you switch. The news report says nothing about how long OpenAI keeps your data, or about HIPAA. So don't send private health data (the law calls it PHI) to Terra until your contract covers it.
- **Healthcare executives:** **Terra** is a cheaper option for routine AI work. OpenAI still says to use its bigger models for the hardest coding. Ask your team which current AI jobs could move to Terra, and what that would save. Ask legal to confirm that your OpenAI agreement covers this new model before anyone uses it with patient data.

### Problems worth taking on

**Price-check one clinical note task on a cheaper model.** *Problem:* Teams that pull data from clinical notes with AI often pay for a top model on every note. Many notes are simple. *Why now:* **GPT-6.1 Terra** (26 October) costs half as much as OpenAI's comparable models. *First step:* Take 200 notes your team already labeled by hand, with patient details removed. Run them through Terra and your current model. Have a data scientist own the test. Success is Terra matching your current model's accuracy within a few points, at a lower total cost.

*Week of 26 October 2026 (ISO week 44). Curated by Mai with Claude.*

## The week in one paragraph

The week's main regulatory move is about software that ships with drugs. On **20 October**, FDA issued **final guidance on prescription drug apps and software** (FDA's term is Prescription Drug Use-Related Software, or PDURS). It finalizes a **September 2023 draft** and tells drug companies when an app's content counts as FDA-required labeling and when it counts as promotion. The main default: what a companion app shows users is **promotional in most cases**. An app goes into the drug's prescribing information only if it is tied to a device or the company shows it is essential or gives a real clinical benefit. Elsewhere, HHS launched a **National Kidney Care Plan** (20 October), and CMS proposed new **pre-eligibility checks for ACA exchange subsidies** (23 October). FDA's own newsroom had no AI-device action this week, and there was no public word on the generative-AI device discussion paper whose comment period closed on 19 October.

## Items

- **[FDA Issues Final Guidance on Prescription Drug Apps and Software](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-issues-final-guidance-prescription-drug-apps-and-software)** `topic:fda`. **Agency:** FDA (CDER/CBER). **Stage:** final guidance, press release dated **20 October 2026**. The Federal Register notice is titled *Regulatory Considerations for Prescription Drug Use-Related Software* (public-inspection document 2026-21516, 21 October) and finalizes the **19 September 2023 draft** ([Alston & Bird weekly review](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/health-care-week-in-review-october-23-2026), 23 October). **Binds:** sponsors of prescription drugs and drug-led combination products that distribute software or app content, or have it distributed for them. **What it says:**
  - The guidance answers three questions: when app content is **FDA-required labeling** and when it is **promotional labeling**, when an app should be referenced in the drug's **prescribing information**, and what sponsors must **submit to FDA** when they distribute app content.
  - **Device-connected apps** (FDA's example is an app paired with an asthma inhaler) should generally be described in the prescribing label.
  - **Standalone apps** (symptom trackers, dose reminders, coaching tools) go on the label only if the sponsor shows the app is **essential for safe and effective use** or gives a **meaningful clinical benefit**. Otherwise, FDA says: "In most cases, content a companion app displays to users will be treated as promotional material."
  - FDA's Jared Seehafer: "Companion apps are quickly moving beyond simple dose reminders toward tools that adapt to each patient."

  The press release does not mention AI, does not list submission mechanics, and gives no effective date. *FDA press release fetched 2026-10-26. The Federal Register PDF was fetched but its text didn't extract, so the guidance text itself was not read. Check it for submission details and changes from the draft.*

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** Under the new **FDA app guidance**, an app gets into a drug's label only with evidence. The drug maker must show the app is needed for safe use or gives a real clinical benefit. Building that proof is your kind of work. If your company has a companion app, find out what outcome data you already collect. Then ask whether it could back a label claim.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** FDA expects companion apps to start "adapting to each patient." Most of what those apps show will count as drug promotion. Promotion has rules and gets filed with FDA. So every screen your app shows is now something your regulatory team may need to review. Log what each user saw and which model or content version made it. You will need that record later.
- **Healthcare executives:** This guidance is final, not a draft. If you sell a drug with an app, decide now whether to seek a label claim for it or treat it as marketing. A label claim takes trial evidence. Marketing means ongoing review of app content. Either way, ask your regulatory lead to read the full guidance. The press release leaves out filing details and dates.

### Problems worth taking on

**Sort your app's screens into "label" and "promotion."** *Problem:* Drug makers with companion apps often have no record of which app screens count as promotion. That leaves them open to FDA findings. *Why now:* **FDA's final app guidance** (20 October) says most app content counts as promotion by default. *First step:* Have regulatory affairs and the app team list every screen and message in one app. Tag each one as "label" or "promotion" under the guidance. Success is a complete list with an owner for each item and a plan for how to file it.

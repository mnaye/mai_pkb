# Healthcare Trends — 2026-W36

*Week of 31 August 2026 (ISO week 36). Curated by Mai with Claude.*

## The week in one paragraph

This was an **enforcement and consent** week, not a funding week. The single largest number was a **$541.5M** Medicare Advantage risk-adjustment settlement by Humana-owned Villages Health — announced 27 August, and notable less for the size than for the mechanism: the company **self-disclosed**, which is what got the number down, and the government is now collecting through a bankruptcy estate. Two state attorneys general opened new fronts the same week, Florida against the PBMs Prime Therapeutics and Express Scripts over alleged price-fixing, and Maryland against Optum over a "defective" Medicaid system. Running underneath all of it, Pew published survey data showing **46% of U.S. adults don't know whether AI has already been used in their care** while **73%** say disclosure is extremely or very important — a consent gap that arrives just as ambient AI becomes standard equipment in the exam room.

## Items

- **Villages Health settles Medicare Advantage upcoding for $541.5M** *(enforcement)* — The Humana-owned Central Florida provider resolved DOJ allegations that from **2020–2024** it submitted false diagnosis codes for MA patients, altering medical records and inserting unsupported codes — sometimes years after the visit — for conditions including severe obesity and immunodeficiency. By 2024, roughly **half** of the company's patient codes lacked supporting documentation. DOJ credited the company for **self-disclosing** the overpayments and cooperating, which reduced the amount; the government is being paid through a claim against the bankruptcy estate, with insurer refunds (Humana ~$151,000; BCBS Florida ~$3M) credited against the total. Announced **2026-08-27**. [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/the-villages-health-medicare-overbilling-settlement-doj-humana/828915/) *(fetched 2026-08-31)*

- **Pew: patients want to be told when AI touches their care — and mostly aren't** *(research / policy)* — Survey of **3,488 U.S. adults**, fielded **22–28 June 2026**, published **2026-08-25**. **73%** say it is extremely or very important that providers disclose AI use; **80%** want to know when AI analyzes scans, makes diagnoses, or explains labs; **72%** for ambient note-taking; **64%** for prescription refills; **56%** even for scheduling. The gap: **53%** report having little or no say over their provider's AI use, **46%** are unsure whether AI has already been used in their care, and among those who did encounter AI in their care, only **22%** understood extremely or very well how it was used. Practical read for anyone deploying clinical AI — disclosure UX is becoming a product requirement ahead of any rule requiring it. [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/08/25/americans-want-transparency-when-ai-is-used-in-their-healthcare/) · coverage: [Healthcare Dive](https://www.healthcaredive.com/news/most-americans-want-providers-disclose-ai-use-survey-pew/828921/) *(fetched 2026-08-31)*

- **Two state AGs open new suits against health-services middlemen** *(regulation / litigation)* — On **2026-08-28**, Florida's attorney general sued **Prime Therapeutics and Express Scripts** over alleged price-fixing, and Maryland sued **Optum**, alleging it defrauded the state Medicaid program with a faulty computer system. Different theories, same target class: the consolidated intermediaries — PBMs and payer-owned services arms — that sit between payers, providers, and patients. State AGs, not federal regulators, are driving this. [Fierce Healthcare — Florida/PBMs](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/florida-ag-sues-prime-therapeutics-express-scripts-alleged-price-fixing) · [Fierce Healthcare — Maryland/Optum](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/maryland-sues-optum-over-defective-computer-system) *(headlines and dates from front-page fetch, 2026-08-31)*

## The week in one paragraph

The week's regulatory signal came from a planning document, not a rule. On **1 October**, the first day of fiscal year 2027, FDA's device center (**CDRH**) published its **FY 2027 guidance agenda**, and the top two final guidances it plans to issue are the ones AI device makers have waited on longest: **lifecycle management for AI-enabled device software functions** (draft since January 2025) and **predetermined change control plans (PCCPs)** (draft since August 2024). The A-list also carries a new draft on **generative-AI conversational devices for mental health**, the first time CDRH has named a generative-AI guidance on its agenda. An agenda is a statement of intent, not a commitment — CDRH finished **7 of 11** listed guidances last year — but it tells AI device teams what to plan for, and the agency is taking comments on its priorities until **30 November**. The generative-AI discussion paper whose comment period closes **19 October** (FDA-2026-N-7874) is still open and gets new weight from this agenda.

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** FDA plans to finish its rules of the road for AI medical devices this year. The **CDRH FY 2027 agenda** puts the AI lifecycle guidance at the top. That guidance covers how you test a model, watch it after launch, and report on it. Read the January 2025 draft now. It is the best guess at what the final will ask your data and test plans to show.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** The second item on the list is **PCCPs**. A PCCP is a plan FDA agrees to up front, so you can update a cleared AI device later without asking again. If you retrain models often, this matters most. Build your retraining and testing steps so they could fit inside such a plan. Also note the new draft on chatbots for mental health. If you build anything like that, expect rules aimed at you.
- **Healthcare executives:** This is a to-do list, not a rule. Last year FDA finished only **7 of 11** items. Still, it tells you where FDA is headed on AI. You can tell FDA what to do first until **30 November** (docket FDA-2012-N-1021). Comments on FDA's generative-AI paper close sooner, on **19 October**. If you sell or plan an AI device, decide this month whether to comment.

### Problems worth taking on

**Get a device update plan ready before the final rule.** *Problem:* Teams with cleared AI devices wait months for FDA each time they want to retrain a model. That delays fixes. *Why now:* The **CDRH agenda** makes final PCCP guidance a top goal for this year. *First step:* Take one model you already have cleared or plan to file. Have the ML lead and the regulatory lead write down which changes you make most often and how you test each one. Success is a draft plan that covers most of last year's updates.

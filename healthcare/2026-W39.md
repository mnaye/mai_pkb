# Healthcare — 2026-W39

*Week of 21 September 2026 (ISO week 39). Curated by Mai with Claude.*

## The week in one paragraph

A busy regulatory week in which the most consequential item is a **refusal**. On **17 September** FDA published its final order **denying** a citizen petition that would have exempted radiology **computer-aided detection, diagnosis and triage software** — the single largest category of AI-enabled medical devices — from 510(k) premarket notification. The agency's answer is that the petition "does not demonstrate that premarket notification is not necessary to assure the safety and effectiveness" of these devices, and that manufacturers **must continue to obtain 510(k) clearance before marketing**. Read against the month's deregulatory mood music — CDRH's own generative-AI discussion paper is out for comment until **19 October** — this is FDA declining to take the off-ramp for the AI device class where it had the most real-world evidence to justify one. Alongside it, CMS reported that **all 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico have applied** to its most-favored-nation Medicaid drug-pricing model, and continued a rapid run of rural-health infrastructure awards.

## Items

- **[FDA denies 510(k) exemption for radiology CAD, CADe/CADx and triage software](https://thefederalregister.org/documents/2026-19074/medical-devices-exemption-from-premarket-notification-radiology-computer-aided-detection-and-or-diagnosis-devices-and-co)** — **Agency:** FDA (CDRH). **Stage:** final order, **published and effective 17 September 2026**. **Citation:** 91 FR 58817, document 2026-19074. **Docket:** FDA-2025-P-5560. **Petitioner:** Nancy Stade, J.D., of Rubrum Advising, LLC, on behalf of **Harrison.ai**. The petition asked FDA to exempt four classifications from premarket notification: **radiological computer-assisted diagnostic software** (§ 892.2060, product code **POK**), **medical image analyzer** (§ 892.2070, **MYN**), **radiological computer-aided triage and notification software** (§ 892.2080, **QAS/QFM**), and **radiological computer-assisted detection and diagnosis software** (§ 892.2090, **QBS/QDQ**). FDA **denied it for all four**, having already responded to the petitioner on **1 April 2026**, finding that the petition "does not demonstrate that premarket notification is not necessary to assure the safety and effectiveness" of these devices and that it failed to adequately address the four standard exemption factors — device safety, established performance characteristics, detectability of harmful changes, and classification stability. **Who it binds:** every manufacturer of these device types — "Manufacturers of the Subject CAD and CADt Devices must continue to submit and receive FDA clearance of a 510(k) submission before marketing their devices." **Why it matters:** these product codes cover the bulk of cleared AI-enabled radiology software, so the decision fixes the regulatory floor for the most commercially mature AI device category and signals that FDA wants premarket review retained precisely where model performance can drift. The ACR had [publicly urged FDA to prioritize safety in reviewing the petition](https://www.acr.org/News-and-Publications/2026/acr-asks-fda-to-prioritize-safety-in-ai-cad-petition-review). *Primary order fetched 2026-09-21 via a Federal Register mirror — federalregister.gov itself returned a 302 to an anti-bot interstitial on this run; the order is also listed on [FDA's CDRH news page](https://www.fda.gov/medical-devices/medical-devices-news-and-events/cdrh-new-news-and-updates) (entry dated 16 Sep).*

## Also noted — regulation & payment

- **[CMS: all 50 states, DC and Puerto Rico apply to the GENEROUS Medicaid drug-pricing model](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-announces-participants-landmark-medicaid-drug-payment-model-bring-down-drug-costs-most)** — **18 September 2026.** The **GENErating cost Reductions fOr U.S. Medicaid (GENEROUS) Model** launched in January 2026 and runs five years; CMS now reports universal application, with **40 states plus Puerto Rico having signed agreements** and the remainder facing a **30 September 2026** deadline. Participating manufacturers "will make covered outpatient drugs available at most-favored nation pricing to participating state Medicaid programs" and "will be invoiced by states for supplemental rebates to effectuate international prices." CMS estimates **$64.3 billion** in savings over ten years. Pegging US Medicaid outpatient drug prices to international reference prices across every state program is the largest payment-side shift of the week and the closest call for this week's top three. *Primary CMS release fetched 2026-09-21.*
- **CDRH generative-AI discussion paper — comment period closes 19 October 2026.** "Considerations for the Regulation of Generative AI-Enabled Medical Devices" (**Docket FDA-2026-N-7874**) asks for early input on risk assessment, premarket evaluation and postmarket monitoring, flagging confabulations that "appear authentic to users," uncertainty in the bounds of intended use, limited visibility into third-party foundation models, and lifecycle performance degradation. Published in **August 2026**, so out of this week's window as news — noted here because the comment deadline is live and it is the policy backdrop to the CAD denial. *Date and docket from secondary coverage ([Mintz, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2791/2026-09-17-fda-flux-september-2026-newsletter)); the primary paper was not fetched — budget spent.*
- **[CMS adds Medicare options for substance use disorder, heart failure and other chronic conditions](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom)** — **15 September 2026**, an expansion of the **ACCESS Model** launching **Spring 2027**.
- **Rural health infrastructure awards continued at pace** — **$104M** to Mississippi (15 Sep, 167 awards), **$167M** to South Carolina and **$50M** to Connecticut (17 Sep), **$74M** to New Mexico (21 Sep), covering telehealth, remote monitoring, mobile care, cybersecurity and maternal health. Individually routine; collectively a sustained push worth watching as a funding stream for digital-health vendors. *From the [CMS newsroom](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom) listing, fetched 2026-09-21.*

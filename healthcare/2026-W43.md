# Healthcare — 2026-W43

*Week of 19 October 2026 (ISO week 43). Curated by Mai with Claude.*

## The week in one paragraph

The week's main regulatory move is about data quality in Medicare Advantage and Part D. On **16 October**, CMS proposed **CMS-4214-P**, which puts three parts of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026 into rule text. The most data-heavy part is the **REAL Health Providers Act**. It makes Medicare Advantage plans check their provider directories, measure how accurate they are, and publish accuracy scores, which CMS will post in machine-readable form from contract year 2029. The rule also sets new reporting and pay rules for pharmacy benefit managers (PBMs) in Part D. It also drops the generic-drug copay to $0 for the poorest dual-eligible enrollees from 2028. Elsewhere, HHS handed CMS the power to exclude providers from federal health programs, and FDA launched a rare-disease drug development framework. FDA's newsroom had no AI-device action this week.

## Items

- **[CMS-4214-P: Implementation of Sections 6219, 6220 and 6224 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-program-implementation-sections-6219-6220-6224-consolidated-appropriations-act-2026-cms)** `topic:payment`. **Agency:** CMS. **Stage:** proposed rule, fact sheet dated **16 October 2026**, with a [CMS press release](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-proposes-reforms-strengthen-transparency-affordability-medicare-advantage-part-d) the same day. **Binds:** Medicare Advantage organizations, Part D sponsors and their PBMs. **What it proposes:**
  - *Section 6220, REAL Health Providers Act (MA provider directories):* directory verification and upkeep standards, a yearly accuracy analysis and report, and public accuracy scores, starting **contract year 2028**. Enrollees who rely on a wrong directory entry get cost-sharing protections. For **contract year 2029**, CMS must post accuracy scores in a **machine-readable format**, and plans must show them prominently in their directories. [Alston & Bird's weekly review](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/health-care-week-in-review-october-16-2026) (16 October) describes updates **every 90 days**. The CMS fact sheet does not give the frequency or the scoring method.
  - *Section 6224, PBM accountability (Part D):* limits on what PBMs and affiliates may be paid for Part D drug services (Alston: tied to fair-market standards rather than drug prices), data reporting to plan sponsors and CMS, transparency on price-guarantee deals, and disclosure to sponsors of certain PBM–manufacturer agreements. Enforcement includes **disgorgement** (paying the money back) to CMS.
  - *Section 6219, Part D cost-sharing:* generic copay drops from **$1 to $0** for full-benefit dual eligibles at or below 100% of the poverty line, for plan years starting **1 January 2028**.

  **Comments:** due 60 days after Federal Register publication. Alston gives **5 p.m. EDT, 15 December 2026** (public-inspection document 2026-21420). The fact sheet gives no savings estimate or count of people affected. *CMS fact sheet fetched 2026-10-19.*

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** **CMS-4214-P** would turn provider directory accuracy into a public score. From 2029, CMS would post the scores in a form computers can read. That gives you a new dataset to compare health plans. It also means your own plan's directory data will be graded. Find out now how your plan checks its directory and how often errors show up.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** Directory checks every 90 days (per Alston's summary) are a job for automation. Think matching provider records across sources, and flagging stale phone numbers or addresses. The PBM part adds new data feeds to plan sponsors and CMS. These are provider and contract data, not patient records. So the privacy load (the law calls patient data PHI) is light. The audit trail still matters, because the scores are public.
- **Healthcare executives:** A bad directory entry would now cost money. Enrollees who trust a wrong listing get cost-sharing protection. PBM deals face new reporting and pay-back penalties. Comments are due **15 December 2026** (per Alston). Decide by then whether to weigh in on how accuracy is scored. Directory rules start in 2028, so budget for the work in next year's plan.

### Problems worth taking on

**Score your own directory before CMS does.** *Problem:* Medicare Advantage plans pay call centers to check provider listings, and wrong listings send members to doctors who aren't taking patients. *Why now:* **CMS-4214-P** would publish accuracy scores and protect members who rely on a wrong listing. *First step:* Pull a random sample of 200 directory entries. Have a data analyst and the network team call or check each one. Success is a baseline error rate you can track each quarter.

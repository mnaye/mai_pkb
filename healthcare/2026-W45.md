## The week in one paragraph

A quiet regulatory week, with one first-of-its-kind approval. On **30 October**, FDA granted **accelerated approval to Inovio's Voceryq**, the first plasmid DNA-based immunotherapy, for adults with recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP), a rare HPV-driven disease treated today by repeated airway surgery. The approval rests on an intermediate endpoint: **7 of 26** heavily operated patients needed no surgery for 12 months. Inovio must run confirmatory trials. The **CY 2027 Physician Fee Schedule final rule**, which sources expected around 1 November, was not found as of this run. There was also no public word on FDA's generative-AI device discussion paper, whose comments closed on 19 October.

## Items

- **[FDA Approves First Plasmid DNA-Based Immunotherapy for Adults with Recurrent Respiratory Papillomatosis](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-plasmid-dna-based-immunotherapy-adults-recurrent-respiratory-papillomatosis)** `topic:fda`. **Agency:** FDA (CBER). **Stage:** accelerated approval, press release dated **30 October 2026**. **Sponsor:** Inovio. **Binds:** Inovio, which "must conduct post-approval clinical trials to verify and describe Voceryq's clinical benefit." **What it says:**
  - Voceryq "delivers two plasmids (small pieces of DNA originally derived from bacteria) into the body's cells." It is indicated for adults with RRP.
  - The basis is a reduction in surgeries, which FDA calls "an intermediate clinical endpoint reasonably likely to predict clinical benefit."
  - Efficacy: "Seven (27%) out of 26 patients who had at least three baseline surgeries did not require any surgery intervention" over 12 months. AJMC reports the 95% CI as 12%–48%, from the RRP-001 study (NCT04398433, 32 adults enrolled).
  - Most common side effects: injection site reactions, headache and fatigue.
  - Per [AJMC](https://ajmc.com/view/fda-approves-first-plasmid-dna-based-immunotherapy-for-adults-with-rrp) (2 November), FDA expects a **draft confirmatory protocol by end of 2026** and a final protocol by mid-2027.

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** FDA approved **Voceryq** based on fewer surgeries, not on a final health outcome. Only 7 of 26 patients (27%) needed no surgery for a year. A small study like this leaves a wide range of doubt. If you work with rare-disease data, this is a real use for your skills. Count surgeries per patient before and after treatment in your own records. That is the same measure FDA used.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** *Nothing actionable this week.* This week's only item is a drug approval. It does not change how you build or run models.
- **Healthcare executives:** **Voceryq** is approved early, on the condition that Inovio proves the benefit in later trials. FDA expects a draft plan for that trial by the end of 2026. If you run an ear, nose and throat or airway surgery program, plan for some patients to try this drug. Tell your pharmacy and payer teams that the evidence is still early.

### Problems worth taking on

**Track surgery counts for RRP patients.** *Problem:* Health systems that treat RRP often can't say how many airway surgeries each patient had last year. That makes it hard to judge a new drug like Voceryq. *Why now:* FDA approved **Voceryq** on 30 October based on cutting the need for surgery. *First step:* Have a data analyst pull RRP patients from your records and count procedures per patient over the past 12 months. Ask the head of ENT (ear, nose and throat) surgery to check the list. Success is a clean baseline you can compare against once patients start the drug.

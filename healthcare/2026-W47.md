*Week of 16 November 2026 (ISO week 47). Curated by Mai with Claude.*

## The week in one paragraph

The regulatory story worth your time is a draft, not a rule. On **29 October** Sen. Bill Cassidy, chair of the Senate HELP Committee, released a discussion draft on AI in health care, the **PATIENT AI Act**. It covers FDA review of AI-enabled software, a CMS coverage pathway for AI-based services, and training for clinicians. Stakeholder feedback is due **1 December 2026**. The first detailed legal read came on **8 November**: the FDA Law Blog finds the FDA title mostly duplicates existing pathways. Its one real new idea is letting sponsors file change-control plans while a submission is still under review. A quiet week for final agency action otherwise. FDA news ran to drug safety (GLP-1 eye warnings, a Zolgensma investigation), seen only in headlines.

## Items

- **[Senate HELP Chairman Releases Draft Legislation on AI in Healthcare — the PATIENT AI Act (AACOM)](https://www.aacom.org/news-reports/news/2026/11/02/senate-help-committee-chairman-releases-draft-legislation-on-ai-in-healthcare)** `topic:fda`. Analysis: [The PATIENT AI Act: A New Prescription for FDA Software Review, or More of the Same? (FDA Law Blog, 8 November)](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/?p=16257). **Who:** Sen. Bill Cassidy, MD (R-LA), Chair of the Senate HELP Committee. **Stage:** discussion draft released **29 October 2026**; not introduced as a bill. **Comment date:** "Stakeholder feedback on the proposal is requested by December 1, 2026" (AACOM, 2 November). **Binds:** nobody yet.
  - **Scope (AACOM):** "a Food and Drug Administration review process for AI-enabled medical software"; "a pathway for the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services to make coverage determinations for AI-based services"; and HHS training resources "to help healthcare professionals effectively implement AI tools."
  - **Sec. 201, component review:** an optional early review of software components. "This new review process would not result in standalone clearance or approval of individual software components."
  - **Sec. 202, PCCPs during review:** sponsors could file predetermined change control plans (PCCPs: pre-agreed rules for how a device's software may change) for changes made while a submission is pending. "This new proposal would allow development and improvements to continue while the submission is pending." The authors warn that without version-control and timing rules it "could become a recipe for confusion rather than faster review."
  - **Sec. 203, third-party review:** extended to low- to moderate-risk software; read literally, the authors say it mainly adds de novo requests. "A broader program is not necessarily a better one."
  - **Sec. 204:** a voluntary FDA pilot to modernize postmarket surveillance of software devices.
  - **Bottom line (Hyman, Phelps & McNamara, 8 November):** "Title II of this draft bill is, in many ways, duplicative of FDA's existing premarket authorization pathways." And: "As a discussion draft, the proposal does not change manufacturers' obligations."

## So what

*For healthcare data scientists, ML engineers in healthcare, and healthcare executives.*

- **Healthcare data scientists:** Nothing actionable this week. The **PATIENT AI Act** is a draft and changes no rules today.
- **ML engineers in healthcare:** Section 202 of the **PATIENT AI Act** draft would let you file a change plan while FDA is still reviewing your device. That plan is called a PCCP: rules, agreed up front, for how your model can be updated. The FDA Law Blog says this would let work on your model go on during review. If that would help you, say so by 1 December. Lawyers at the FDA Law Blog warn it needs clear version rules. Write down how you track model versions now, so your comment is concrete.
- **Healthcare executives:** Sen. Cassidy wants feedback by **1 December 2026**. The draft touches FDA review, Medicare coverage of AI services, and staff training. It is not law and changes nothing yet. One legal review calls the FDA part mostly a repeat of current rules. Decide this week if your group will comment, and on which part. Medicare coverage of AI may matter more to your budget than the FDA part.

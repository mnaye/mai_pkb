# Research Skill — Healthcare Trends (regulation-first)

A focused source guide for the `healthcare/` area. The weekly brief loads this when researching healthcare.

## Goal

Surface the most notable **new** healthcare developments from the past 7 days, with **changes in healthcare regulation as the top priority**.

**Priority 1 — Regulation & policy (lead with this).** Spend most of the area's budget here:

- **FDA** — device/AI guidance (draft or final), clearances and approvals of note, recalls and safety communications, advisory-committee outcomes.
- **CMS** — proposed and final rules, Medicare/Medicaid coverage and reimbursement changes, new payment models, CMMI announcements.
- **HHS / OCR / ASTP-ONC** — HIPAA and privacy rulemaking, enforcement actions, information blocking, interoperability and health-IT certification (TEFCA).
- **Legislation** — bills moving in Congress, and state laws with national reach (AI in utilization review, telehealth licensure, privacy).
- **International** — EU AI Act as applied to health, EU MDR/IVDR, EMA, UK MHRA — when the change affects US-facing companies.
- **Enforcement & litigation** — FTC health-privacy actions, DOJ/OIG, court decisions that change what's permitted.

**Priority 2 — Industry & market.** Funding rounds, M&A, product launches, provider/payer/pharma moves — favor items that turn on a regulatory change.

**Priority 3 — Research.** Clinical and translational studies with real-world impact, especially evidence that feeds an approval or coverage decision.

## How to research

1. **Web-search regulation first** (past 7 days), e.g.:
   - `FDA guidance AI medical device 2026`, `CMS final rule 2026`
   - `HIPAA rule change`, `healthcare AI state law`, `Medicare reimbursement rule`
2. **Fetch the primary regulatory page** — the FDA/CMS/HHS announcement, the Federal Register entry, or the bill text — before any secondary coverage.
3. Only after regulation is covered, spend what's left of the budget on industry and research.

## What to capture per item

- **Name / headline** (bold)
- One-line description — what it is and why it matters
- For a regulatory item, also note: **which agency**, **what stage** (draft guidance / proposed rule / final rule / enforcement), **effective or comment-close date**, and **who it binds**.
- **Link** to the fetched source
- Category tag where useful (regulation / funding / research / product)

## Guardrails

- **Only include articles you actually fetched** — no invented links, figures, or quotes.
- Prefer the primary regulatory document (the agency announcement, the Federal Register notice, the bill) over reporting about it.
- Don't dress up routine agency activity as a change — a single clearance or a scheduled report is only notable if it sets a precedent or shifts what companies must do.
- Be precise with dates and numbers (comment deadlines, effective dates, payment rates) — quote them only from the fetched source.
- If a genuinely bigger non-regulatory story breaks (a major approval-independent trial result, a landmark acquisition), it can still win the week — regulation is the priority, not a quota.

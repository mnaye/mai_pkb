# Research Skill — Southeast Asian Food & Culture

A focused source guide for the `sea-food-culture/` area. The weekly brief loads this when researching Southeast Asian food and culture.

## Goal

Surface the most notable **new** food-and-culture stories from the past 7 days across Southeast Asia — new restaurant openings, dish trends, culinary heritage, food-industry moves, festivals, and travel-culture coverage. Favor stories with a clear regional tie (Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Vietnam, Philippines, Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar, Brunei, Timor-Leste).

## Prioritization — go off the beaten path

Deliberately weight toward what a Western reader is **least likely to already know**:

- **Lesser-covered countries first.** Prioritize Laos, Cambodia, Myanmar, Brunei, Timor-Leste, and the Philippines, plus rural/eastern Indonesia (Sulawesi, Maluku, Papua, Nusa Tenggara), over the usual Thailand / Vietnam / Singapore headliners.
- **Regional & indigenous dishes over national icons.** Favor a specific province's or ethnic group's specialty over the dishes already famous in the West (pad thai, pho, nasi goreng, satay). Surface the unfamiliar version, ingredient, or technique.
- **Heritage, foraged, fermented, and home/village cooking** that rarely reaches Western menus.
- Use the famous stuff only as a hook when it opens onto something less known (e.g. a regional variant, a disappearing technique, an origin story).

When two stories are equally notable, pick the one most Westerners have **never been exposed to**.

## How to research

1. **Web-search first** to find recent stories (past 7 days), using queries that pair a focus with the region, e.g.:
   - `Laos / Cambodia / Myanmar / Timor-Leste cuisine 2026`
   - `regional Indonesian food Sulawesi / Maluku / Papua`
   - `Filipino regional dish heritage`, `indigenous Southeast Asian food`
   - `SEA street food trend`, `Michelin Southeast Asia`, `food festival [country]`
   - Lean the queries toward the lesser-known countries and provinces above rather than the usual headliners.
2. **Then fetch (scrape) the actual article pages** from the priority sources below to pull the real title, summary, and link. Never cite a source you didn't fetch.

## What to capture per item

- **Name / headline** (bold)
- One-line description — what it is and why it matters
- **Link** to the fetched source article
- Country/region tag where useful

## Guardrails

- **Only include articles you actually fetched** — no invented links, titles, or quotes.
- Prefer primary reporting over aggregators; if two sources cover the same story, cite the original.
- Skip listicles with no time-sensitive news value ("10 best noodles of all time") unless newly published and genuinely notable.
- Keep summaries neutral and factual; attribute curation to **Mai with Claude**.
